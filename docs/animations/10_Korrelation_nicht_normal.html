High correlation without normal distributions

Pearson's correlation does not need normal data. Pick a clearly non-normal distribution for x, set the target correlation ρ and draw samples: the cloud stays roughly linear and r is high, although both histograms are far from a bell.

The correlation coefficient measures how well the points follow a straight line. It says nothing about the shape of the marginal distributions. Even with a strongly skewed x, y = ρ·x + noise lies close to a line.

Normality matters elsewhere: for exact tests and confidence intervals of r in small samples, and because a few extreme points can move r a lot. Try the lognormal distribution with a small n and draw several samples.
