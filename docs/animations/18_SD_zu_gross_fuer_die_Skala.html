What goes wrong. Mean ± SD describes a roughly bell-shaped distribution that can reach far in both directions. Most counts and patient-reported scores cannot. They have a floor (no pain, no fall, no needle), sometimes a ceiling (10 on a pain scale, 12 prescribed sessions), and real patients pile up at these limits or split into two groups. The mean is pulled towards the tail, and the SD adds the length of the tail to the width of the bulk. A reader who sees “2.4 (3.1)” pictures a bell curve and wrongly assumes that values below 0 exist.

The SD is not free on a bounded scale. For data between a minimum m and a maximum M with mean x̄, the variance cannot exceed (M − x̄)(x̄ − m). This is the Bhatia–Davis inequality (Bhatia and Davis, American Mathematical Monthly 2000). For the sample SD the bound gets the factor n/(n − 1) under the root. An SD close to the cap means that nearly all values sit at the two ends of the scale. An SD above the cap is impossible and points to a typing error or to a different scale than stated.

Counts and durations. Here the SD is often as large as the mean or larger, because many small values meet a few very large ones. The few large values dominate the mean, and even more the SD, because it squares the distances. Altman and Bland (BMJ 1996) described how to detect skewness from such summary numbers. The card “Bell curve outside the scale” gives the same information as a share instead of a rule.

What to report instead. Show the distribution, either as a histogram or as the number of patients per value. Or give the median with the quartiles, the minimum and the maximum, and say how many patients sit at the floor or at the ceiling. Mean and SD can stay in the table for comparisons, but the reader needs the shape next to them. The SD stays a valid number, yet it describes such data poorly.

Try this. (1) Pain after treatment: the band around the mean leaves the scale on the left. (2) Responders and non-responders: the SD comes close to the largest possible SD, and the mean sits where almost nobody is. (3) Switch to “Same protocol for everybody” or “Before treatment”: now mean and SD describe the data well. (4) Press “New sample” several times and watch how stable the picture is.
