Do it again and again: how much does the number vary?

Each dot below the axis is one research department of 1000 researchers, placed at the number of false positives it got by chance. The orange bars show the share of departments per count (a density, so the total area is 1) and appear after 10 departments. The line is what theory predicts (binomial distribution, n = 1000). It stays the same, the bars come closer to it with every department.

Nobody in this department has an effect to find: every orange duck is a false positive. With a 4 % chance per study, about 40 out of 1000 researchers will report something “significant” by chance alone. Getting 47 would not be unusual, getting 137 would be extremely unusual.
