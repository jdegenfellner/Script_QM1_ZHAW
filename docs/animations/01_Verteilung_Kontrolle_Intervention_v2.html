Control vs. intervention: pain intensity in two groups

Outcome: pain intensity on a numeric rating scale from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst pain). Higher is worse. Set the distribution of each group yourself: mean, spread (SD), skewness and the share of patients with no pain at all (a spike at 0, "zero inflation"). Both groups start from the control distribution. Press Apply intervention and the intervention group moves to the settings you chose for it.

Mean, SD and skewness describe the curve of the patients with pain before it is cut off at 0 and 10. The table at the bottom shows the resulting values of the whole group, including patients with no pain. The settings of the intervention group take effect when you press Apply intervention.

The height of a curve is a density, not a probability. What counts is the area under the curve. The bar at 0 is different: its height is the probability of exactly no pain (full plot height = 100 %). Curve area and bar together always add up to 1 for each group. Skewness 0 gives a symmetric (bell-shaped) curve, positive skewness a long tail towards severe pain, negative skewness a long tail towards mild pain. A pure shift changes mean, median and mode by the same amount and leaves the shape unchanged. With different shapes the three can move by different amounts, or even in different directions.

Mode: peak of the curve. It is 0 if the bar at 0 is higher than the probability mass around the peak (a one-point-wide interval).

How different are two random patients?

Draw one patient at random from the control group and one from the intervention group, independently of each other. How likely is it that they differ by at least d points of pain? The difference of two random patients is itself a random variable. Its curve is shown below, and the probability is the shaded area. The slider sets d.
