Q-Q plots: what do samples of different distributions look like?

Left: the true distribution (and, in orange, the histogram of the current sample). Right: the normal Q-Q plot of that sample. Set the sample size and draw new samples as often as you like.

Band around the line:

How to read it: If the points follow the straight line and stay inside the band, the sample is compatible with a normal distribution. The line goes through the first and third quartile of the sample. The bands are pointwise: a 99 % band is wider than a 95 % band, and even for normal data a few points fall outside the 95 % band by chance.

Try this: With n = 10 even normal data wiggle a lot and skewed data are hard to recognise. With n = 500 the pattern of the skewed distributions is unmistakable. Switch on the grey overlay to see how much the Q-Q plot of normal data varies from sample to sample.
