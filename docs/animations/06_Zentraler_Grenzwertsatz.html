Central limit theorem: means of samples look normal

Pick a population, pick a sample size n, then draw samples. Each sample gives one mean X̄. Watch what the collection of means looks like.

Whatever the shape of the population (skewed, flat, two humps, a coin), the means of many samples pile up in a bell shape around the true mean μ. For large n, X̄ is approximately N(μ, σ²/n).

The spread of X̄ is the standard error σ/√n. It shrinks when n grows, but slowly: four times as many observations halve it. How large n has to be depends on the population. Try the very skewed one: n = 30 is not enough yet.
