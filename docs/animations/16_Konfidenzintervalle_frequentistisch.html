Confidence intervals: what “95 %” really means

The frequentist idea. The true mean μ is a fixed number. The interval is what changes from sample to sample. “95 %” is a statement about the procedure: in the long run, about 95 % of the intervals built this way contain μ. A single interval either contains μ or it does not. Once drawn, there is no probability left in it.

Try this. Draw 100 intervals at once with the 95 % level and count the red ones. They are not “rare errors”: about 5 of 100 miss. Then raise the level to 99 % and note that the intervals become wider. With the skewed distribution and n = 5 to 10, fewer than 95 % of the t-intervals contain μ. With a larger n the share comes closer to 95 % (central limit theorem).
