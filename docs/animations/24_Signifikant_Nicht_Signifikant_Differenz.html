“Significant” versus “not significant”: is the difference itself clear?

Two independent estimates A and B, for example the effect of a treatment in two groups or two studies. Each estimate gets its own p-value against zero. The third row compares A with B directly. Change the estimates and their standard errors and watch what happens to the three p-values and intervals.

p-values and intervals use the normal approximation with the standard errors set above: z = estimate / SE, and the standard error of the difference is √(SE_A² + SE_B²). This assumes that A and B are independent. The p-value is the probability, under “true value = 0” and the model assumptions, of an estimate at least as extreme as the one observed. The labels in the cards only show on which side of the chosen α each p-value lies. They are not a recommendation to decide that way. Source of the idea and the first example: Gelman and Stern (2006), The American Statistician 60(4):328–331, doi 10.1198/000313006X152649.
