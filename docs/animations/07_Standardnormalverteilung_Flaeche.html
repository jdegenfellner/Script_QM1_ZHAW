Standard normal distribution: area between two limits

Drag the limits a and b along the curve (or use the arrow keys when a limit is selected). Whole numbers snap into place. The area under the curve between the limits is the probability P(a ≤ X ≤ b).

The x-axis is measured in standard deviations from the mean 0. The height f(x) is the density, not a probability. Only the area under the curve is a probability, and the whole area is 1.

Try the rule of thumb: between −1 and 1 lie about 68 % of the values, between −2 and 2 about 95 %, between −3 and 3 about 99.7 %.
