Which random process generates each row?
RowProcess used for the simulation

Click again and again: almost every cell changes, but around the same centre. Nothing in a published table is “the” truth about a group. It is one realisation of a random process, and a new trial from the same populations would print slightly different numbers.

Look at the medians and quartiles of the two skewed rows and at the small percentages (“< 1”, “1”) in the confidence rows: rare categories and skewed durations fluctuate much more than the mean of a roughly symmetric scale.

Why this p-value column is wrong. In a randomised trial the two groups differ at baseline by chance only, so testing the baseline table does not answer any question (the CONSORT statement advises against it). Here the switch makes this visible: both groups are now drawn from one pooled population, so every null hypothesis is true and the true differences are exactly zero.

Nevertheless, about 5 % of the p-values fall below 0.05, and with 11 tests per table, about every second table (43 %) contains at least one “significant” baseline difference. Keep clicking and watch the counters.
